Meta's Muse models, hunting for new world records in mathematics.

An AI research team writes programs, looks at pictures of its own results, and keeps improving until it beats the best answer humans have published.

What problem is this?

Spread points as far apart as possible on a sphere. With a few points it's easy. With 150+ points in four dimensions, nobody knows the best arrangement. Mathematicians keep a public table of the best known answers.

Why it matters. The same maths decides how well signals can be told apart in radio, Wi-Fi and error-correcting codes.

How we score. We measure how close the two nearest points are (the "largest cosine"). Lower is better. Every result is checked independently to 50 digits.

How it works

1 · Muse Spark writes code

Eight workers each take a parent program, its picture and the lab's strategy, and write a new solver.

2 · Safe testing

Each program runs in an isolated sandbox with a time limit, and a strict checker scores the answer.

3 · Pictures, not just numbers

Every result is rendered (contact graphs, slack profiles, packings), and workers study the picture before writing the next program.

4 · Muse Wonder leads

Every few rounds the principal investigator reads the top programs and the best picture, diagnoses the stall and assigns new directions.
